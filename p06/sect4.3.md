Summary by Leo C. 

# Question 1

I have mixed experience for both individual programming and programming in a small group. For introductory classes like lower division CS classes, we mostly learn the basics of C++ and implement the knowledge from class to solve problems, these classes usually have very clear instruction as guidances. I am expected to figure out the problem on my own and submit my own code for the sake of practice and preparing for both exams and future job prep. 
I also have taken courses that make students work in a small group ( =< 3 students). For example, CS170 is the operating system class that was taught by Professor Wolski, where we had to divide work, communicate with each other, and help each other when someone was stuck. I think these group projects are closer to what software development is like in industry, although the teams and projects in school are usually much smaller. Like what the paper said, the instruction for this class is more blurry than others, we would have to search up different functions online or checking man page of the Unix interface in order to finish the lab on time. 


# Question 2
One thing that was surprising for software engineers is how much teamwork is involved in the industry. Working on a project is not like finishing a CS course lab where you finish everything yourself without referencing other people's code, instead, you need to work closely with other people and actively communicate with them and ask (the right) questions. 
They also found it very surprising that asking the right person when running into a problem is very important working in the industry as well. During school, it is obvious that either the professor or the TAs have the answers to all the questions related to the class, but in real world, since work(potentially a large codebase) has been divided into portions for different people to work on, it is important for a developer to find the right person to ask their questions. It is their own duty to figure out who understands certain part better than all others and ask the right question to the right person. 

# Question 3
From the paper, one suggestion to students was involve and participate in more group projects in school to prepare for the real-world programing problems. This can help students get better at communicating, dividing work, managing time, and solving problems together. 
Another suggestion was students should learn what questions should be ask for which person. Learning who is responsible for what part of the project is the key to effective teamwork, since programing projects in the industry involves dividing work into smaller portions and some people might not be familiar with the part you have questions about. 
For my own idea, I think it would be very useful for students to work in a even larger group (larger than current size of 6 students in this course), each group should work on a bigger project that involves developing of a more complicated app, where different students or groups are responsible for different parts of the same application. This would make the project depend more on teamwork, similar to industry. At the same time, students will have more experience using GitHub for working in a larger team that better prepare them for tasks in the industry.
